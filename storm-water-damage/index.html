Storm water enters a house in several ways at once. Rain blown sideways reaches windows and doors, runoff pools against a garage door, a roof gives way at a weak spot and a drain backs up. Emergency storm water damage service in Dana Point, CA treats all of these as one cleanup problem.

This page is about what happens after the storm. For creek or tide flooding with rising water, flood damage restoration is the closer match.

How Storm Water Gets Into Dana Point Homes

Southern California storms are rare but intense. The National Weather Service issued a flood watch for the region in August 2023 during a tropical storm, an unusual event for a coast that averages about 12.4 inches of rain a year according to the city's own climate summary.

Houses here are not built for sustained rain. Low garage thresholds, patio doors on slab edges and older window seals all give water a way in. Homes on the bluffs take wind-driven rain, while lower streets can see runoff gather against doors.

Storm Safety Steps Before the Cleanup

  • Stay out of standing water near outlets, panels and cords. If the panel is wet, call the utility or 911.
  • Do not walk through moving water outside. Depth and current are easy to misjudge.
  • Keep children and pets out of any water that came from a drain or the street.
  • Take photos before moving anything, then call the 24/7 line.

What the Crew Does

1

Extract the water

Pumps and wet vacuums remove standing water from the garage, living space and low entries.

2

Sort the materials

Carpet pad, insulation and soft drywall that soaked up runoff are usually removed. Hard surfaces are washed.

3

Sanitize

Floors and walls that touched street or drain water are cleaned and treated.

4

Dry and check

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until moisture meters show dry readings.

What Changes the Cost

The scope depends on the entry point, how deep and how long the water stood, how much is contaminated, how many rooms were hit and how many drying days follow. A garage-only event is simpler than water that reached carpet and cabinets. Scope is explained before work starts, with no price over the phone.

Storm Damage and Your Insurance

Rain that comes in through a damaged roof or window is not the same as flood. A standard homeowners policy often treats wind-driven rain and flood differently, and flood usually needs separate coverage. Ask your agent which applies, and document the water line on walls before cleanup.

What the Record Shows Around Dana Point

The city's public works page reports revised FEMA flood map panels along San Juan Creek. The flood hazard page adds that high tides, storm surge and heavy rainfall can compound risk because of Dana Point's position on the Pacific.

San Juan Capistrano, a few miles inland, has a history of creek flooding. Trabuco Creek caused evacuations there on December 22, 2010. Dana Point homes near the creek mouth should check the current FEMA panel instead of relying on an old lender letter.

Mistakes to Avoid After a Storm

  • Running a household vacuum on standing water. Use only equipment rated for it.
  • Leaving soaked carpet in place to dry on its own.
  • Waiting for the sun. Damp materials can start growing mold within one to two days according to EPA guidance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is emergency storm water damage service available in Dana Point?

Yes, including during and right after storms and on holidays. Same-day response is offered when crews are available, and dispatch talks you through safe steps first.

Is storm water considered dirty water?

Usually. Runoff picks up soil, oil and bacteria, so porous materials it touched are often removed.

Does insurance cover storm water damage?

Coverage varies. Wind-driven rain through a damaged opening is often treated differently from flooding. Check your policy.

Can you do the roof repair?

No. The crew handles interior water and drying. A roofer should repair the roof.

What if the water is still rising?

Move to higher ground and call 911 if anyone is at risk. Call the restoration line once it is safe.
