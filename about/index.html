Dana Point Water Damage Pros is a 24/7 dispatch line for water damage restoration based at 34085 Pacific Coast Highway in Dana Point. When water gets into a home, you call one number, describe what is wet and a crew is arranged. Same-day response is offered when crews are available.

The site is written by the company as a whole, not by any one person. We do not claim awards, review counts or years in business here, and we keep our statements to what we can explain: how the work is done, what standards it follows and what you can expect to be told before work starts.

What We Do

Six services, from emergency water removal and water mitigation to flood damage restoration, water damage cleanup, water damage repair and mold remediation. Crews use extraction units, moisture meters, thermal imaging, air movers and dehumidifiers, and they follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage and S520 mold standards as industry references.

What We Do Not Do

We do not give health advice, we do not predict what an insurer will pay and we do not promise a specific arrival time. We do not repair roofs or replace plumbing, and we will tell you when one of those is the real fix. Our blog explains common situations in plain words, with local sources where we found them.

What the Standards Mean for You

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the industry reference for professional water damage restoration. It sorts water into three categories by contamination, describes how to dry structures and sets out how readings show progress. S520 is the matching reference for mold remediation, and it covers containment, removal and verification. We name them as industry standards that crews work from, not as certificates we hold.

In practice, that means you hear which category the water is, you see readings through the job and you are told what comes out and why. Dry means readings match a reference from an unaffected part of your home, not that surfaces feel dry.

What to Expect on the Phone

The dispatcher asks for the address, where the water is, where it came from and whether it is still running. If the water looks dirty or smells like sewage, say so, since that changes the crew and the gear. You will be told how to shut off the water, what to leave alone and what to photograph while you wait.

If a plumber or roofer is needed first, you will hear that too. Restoration crews handle the water that got out, and other trades fix what let it out. Being clear about that boundary saves time and avoids paying for work that cannot hold.

Why a Single Dispatch Line

Water damage does not arrive on a schedule, so a single line that answers at any hour is the simplest design. You do not need to know whether your problem is mitigation, cleanup or flood work. You describe it, and dispatch routes it. The same line handles questions after the job, such as what to keep for your insurer or when repairs can start.

Licensing and Records

Check any contractor's license on the California Contractors State License Board website, and ask for it in writing before you sign anything. Keep your policy, photos, moisture readings and written scope together, since they answer most questions from insurers and buyers. Our contact page has the address, email and phone.
