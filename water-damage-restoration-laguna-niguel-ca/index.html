A Master-Planned City With Aging Plumbing

Laguna Niguel grew from the old Rancho Niguel land grant. In 1959 the Laguna Niguel Corporation bought the land to build one of California's early master-planned communities, and the first town plan covered 7,100 acres. AVCO Community Developers expanded the project from 1971, so much of the housing dates from the 1970s and 1980s.

Homes of that age have original supply lines, water heaters, shutoff valves and appliance hoses that are now decades old. Those parts, not storms, cause most calls here. A corroded line behind a wall can run for days before a stain appears, and the first sign is often a soft baseboard or a musty closet.

When Water Is Spreading in Laguna Niguel

If a pipe has burst or a water heater has failed, the next hour matters more than the next day. Dispatch asks where the water started, how far it has traveled and whether anyone can reach the main shutoff, and those answers decide what equipment is loaded. Calls at night, on weekends and on holidays go through the same line.

Before We Arrive: If Water Is Still Spreading

1

Stop the source

Close the main valve, usually near the street or beside the garage, or the shutoff at the fixture.

2

Power

Turn off power to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely. Otherwise call the electric utility.

3

Lift and move

Lift small furniture, rugs and electronics onto dry surfaces and take photos before moving anything.

4

People and pets

Keep children and pets out of the water, and stay out of any room with a sagging ceiling.

5

Call the line

Call the 24/7 line and describe what is wet. Dispatch will tell you what to leave alone.

Creeks, Lakes and Canyon Drainage

Salt Creek runs through the southern part of the city and Sulphur Creek drains much of the northern half. Laguna Niguel Lake was formed by damming Sulphur Creek, and Aliso and Wood Canyons Wilderness Park covers more than 4,000 acres along the western border. Most homes sit well away from these channels, but lots near the canyons and creeks can see runoff in a heavy storm.

The city's Crown Valley Parkway corridor and the Interstate 5 interchanges at Avery Parkway and Crown Valley Parkway are the routes crews use from Dana Point. Moulton Niguel Water District, formed in 1960, supplies the area, and a pressure problem or main break on the street is a utility matter, not a restoration job.

What Crews Do Here First

In a typical Laguna Niguel job the crew finds the source, extracts standing water, measures moisture with meters and thermal imaging, then places air movers and dehumidifiers. Wet drywall and pad that cannot be dried come out, and the structure is checked again until readings hold. The work follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard.

After drying, water damage cleanup handles debris and odor, and water damage repair rebuilds what was removed. If water sat long enough for dark spots to form, mold remediation is the follow-up, and the leak gets fixed first.

ZIP Codes and Nearby Cities

Laguna Niguel's main ZIP code is 92677, and the crews also serve neighboring Aliso Viejo and Laguna Hills for water damage. Coverage in each is by request, so call and give the address. Coastal customers just south, in Dana Point, are the home base, and San Juan Capistrano sits to the northeast.

Insurance questions come up on most calls. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by homeowners policies, gradual leaks often are not, and floods usually need separate coverage. Ask your insurer, keep photos and keep the written scope.
