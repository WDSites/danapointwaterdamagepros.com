Washing Machine Hose Leak: First Steps for Laguna Niguel and Dana Point Homes

A hose that bursts under pressure can empty gallons a minute. Stop the water at the wall, then check what it reached.

By Dana Point Water Damage Pros · Updated October 29, 2026

Emergency Dispatch
(949) 670-0939
☎ Call Now

Washing machine supply hoses sit under constant pressure, and they wear out. Rubber hoses crack, fittings corrode and the connection to the wall valve can loosen. When one fails, water sprays across the laundry room and runs into adjoining rooms before anyone notices. This guide covers what to do and what cleanup involves.

If it is happening now, call the 24/7 line while you act on the first two steps. Same-day response is offered when crews are available.

Stop the Water at the Wall

Behind the washer, two small valves feed the hot and cold hoses. Turn both clockwise until they stop. If you cannot reach them because of spray or a tight space, close the main shutoff for the house. Then unplug the washer if you can do so without standing in water.

If the hose failed while you were away, the water may have run for hours. In that case, the damage is likely larger than the spill you see, and the crew will check the subfloor and walls below and beside the laundry area.

What Water From a Washer Carries

A burst supply hose releases clean water at first. An overflow or drain failure can release gray water that contains detergent and soil. The crew checks which one it was, because the category affects what can be dried and what must be removed. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard guides that call.

How Water Reaches Laguna Niguel Homes

Moulton Niguel Water District was formed in 1960 when eight local ranchers set out to bring a more reliable water supply to the area. The district provides water, wastewater and recycled water, and its customer base grew from 33,495 by 1972 to roughly 160,000 around 2000.

Supply pressure is part of why hoses fail. A line that is under steady pressure for years wears at the rubber and the crimps. In a city where many homes date to the 1970s and 1980s, original hoses and valves are not unusual, and replacing them is a small job compared with the water damage they can cause.

What Cleanup Involves

The crew extracts standing water, then uses moisture meters and thermal imaging to find water that moved out of sight. Wet baseboards may be removed so walls can dry, and wet carpet pad is usually discarded. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days. Emergency water removal is the first stage, and water damage cleanup follows.

If the water reached a second-floor ceiling or walls below, the crew may open a section to dry insulation. After drying, water damage repair rebuilds and repaints.

How to Tell a Hose Is Aging

Look at the hoses with a flashlight. A bulge near the fitting, a crack in the outer rubber, rust stains on the connection or a hose that feels stiff and brittle are all signs it is near the end. Hoses are cheap compared with the floor they protect, and many plumbers suggest replacing them every few years even when they look fine.

Listen for drips after a cycle and check for dampness on the wall behind the machine. A slow weep can run unseen for weeks, which is the kind of leak that insurers often treat differently from a sudden burst. Catching it early keeps the repair small.

Moving the Washer Safely

If you need to pull the washer out to reach the valves, turn off the water first and unplug the machine. Pull it straight out without crushing the hoses, and set a towel under the connections to catch drips. Check the floor behind it for stains or soft spots, which can show an older leak. If anything looks wet, stop and call for readings before you push the machine back.

Preventing the Next One

  • Replace rubber hoses with braided stainless steel hoses.
  • Check hoses every year for bulges, cracks or rust at the fittings.
  • Leave several inches between the washer and the wall so hoses do not kink.
  • Turn off the wall valves when you are away for long trips.
  • Consider a drain pan under a washer on an upper floor.

Insurance and Next Steps

A sudden hose failure is often treated as sudden and accidental water damage, but policies vary and some exclude damage from long, slow leaks. Photograph everything before cleanup, save the failed hose and keep receipts. If you live in Mission Viejo or San Juan Capistrano, the same steps apply.

Local Questions

Where are the washer shutoff valves?

Behind the washer on the wall, one for hot and one for cold. Turn both clockwise to close them.

Is a stainless braided hose better than rubber?

Many plumbers prefer braided stainless hoses because they resist bursting, but any hose should be checked yearly and replaced when worn.

Will the water reach the neighbors in a townhome?

It can. Water travels along floors and walls, so tell your neighbors and your association early.

Do I need to keep the failed hose?

Yes if possible. Your insurer or plumber may want to see it.
