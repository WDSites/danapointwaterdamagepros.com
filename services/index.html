Water damage restoration is not one job. It is a sequence of jobs, and the right one depends on where you are in it. A burst pipe at midnight needs extraction. A flooded garage after a storm needs contaminated water handled safely. A stained ceiling a month later needs a repair. This page explains each service in plain terms so you can see which fits your situation.

All six are available through the same dispatch line in Dana Point, open 24/7, with same-day response when crews are available. Scope and price are explained before work starts, and a crew will tell you if a plumber, roofer or other trade is needed first.

Emergency Water Removal

Emergency water removal is the first call when water is standing in your home. Crews use truck-mounted and portable extraction units to pull water from floors, then check what is wet beneath the surface. Speed matters because water soaks into drywall, pad and subfloor within hours.

Water Mitigation

Water mitigation stops damage from spreading. The crew controls the source, measures moisture with meters and thermal imaging and sets air movers and dehumidifiers. Mitigation is about the first 72 hours, and it follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration.

Water Damage Cleanup

Water damage cleanup handles what the water left behind. Debris is removed, surfaces are cleaned, odors are treated and unsalvageable materials are discarded. Cleanup often overlaps with mitigation, and it ends when the space is ready for repair.

Flood Damage Restoration

Flood damage restoration covers water that comes from outside, including storm runoff, high tides and sewage backups. That water is treated as contaminated, so crews use protective gear, remove porous materials and sanitize before drying. Flood coverage is usually separate from homeowners insurance.

Water Damage Repair

Water damage repair is the rebuild. Drywall, insulation, trim, flooring, paint and cabinets are replaced or restored once readings show the structure is dry. Repairs are not started on a wet structure, because rebuilding over moisture hides the problem.

Mold Remediation

Mold remediation is a secondary service that follows moisture problems. It involves containment, removal of affected materials and cleaning, following the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard. The leak is fixed first. This page does not make health claims, and questions about health belong with a physician.

How a Typical Job Moves Through the Services

Most jobs start with extraction and mitigation, run through cleanup and end with repair. A crew first finds the source and stops further damage, then pulls out standing water. Moisture mapping shows where water traveled, and drying equipment runs until readings reach the dry standard. Only then does the rebuild begin.

Some jobs skip steps. A small leak caught quickly may need only mitigation and a patch. A flood may need extensive cleanup before any drying. The crew explains which steps your job needs and why.

What to Do Before You Call

  • Stop the source if it is safe to do so.
  • Stay out of standing water near outlets and panels.
  • Keep children and pets away from wet or contaminated areas.
  • Take photos of the damage before moving anything.
  • Have the address and a short description ready.

Choosing a Company for Any of These Services

Ask whether the crew follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water and S520 for mold, and ask to see moisture readings as the job proceeds. Ask for the scope in writing and check any contractor license with the California Contractors State License Board. A company that refuses to explain scope and price before work starts is a company to avoid.

Ask how the crew handles contaminated water, how drying will be verified and what trades are separate from the work. Good answers are specific. Vague answers about speed and quality are not a substitute for readings and a written scope.

Insurance Notes

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by homeowners insurance, gradual leaks often are not and floods usually need separate coverage. Policies differ, so ask your insurer and keep photos, receipts and the written scope. We do not promise what an insurer will pay.
